# CSoT'2026 — CAIC Summer of Tech

## Product purpose

A 5-week, multi-track tech program run by CAIC at IIT Delhi. The site exists for one job: get a fresher to look at the 14 tracks, find the two or three that excite them, and register. Inter-IIT Tech Meet preparation underneath, but the surface message is "try things, learn by building, no prior experience required."

## Register

**brand** for the landing and track cards (this IS the front door of an initiative — its job is to make students lean in). **product** for the registration form and dashboard (these serve a job, get out of the way).

## Users

- Primary: 1st-year and 2nd-year students at IIT Delhi, mostly unsure what part of tech they want to explore. Curious, easily intimidated by "advanced", time-poor (other clubs, courses, life).
- Secondary: 3rd/4th-years who want to onboard onto Inter-IIT contingents. Already opinionated, scanning for the track that matches their existing skills.
- Tertiary: club leads from ARIES, BnC, Dev, Aero, Robo, ACES, IGTS, Eco, ANCC, PAC who want their track to look serious and worth running.

## Tone

Direct, warm, slightly editorial. Speaks like a senior whom you'd actually trust, not like a corporate prospectus or a hyped-up bootcamp ad. No emojis as decoration. No "🚀 unlock your potential". No exclamation marks beyond at most one per page.

## Anti-references

Hard no on:
- Y-Combinator-style cream + black-serif landing pages (too saturated in 2026).
- Generic "AI lab" dark mode with neon green / cyan accents.
- IIT-blue + golden-yellow college-website aesthetic.
- Identical icon-and-heading card grids (Bootstrap-marketing template look).
- Hero with a giant gradient blob behind a giant headline.
- "Powered by [logos]" bars.
- Stock photography of students with laptops.

## Strategic principles

1. **The tracks ARE the page.** No carousel, no "as featured in", no testimonials. The landing exists to put the 14 tracks in front of the user as fast as possible with enough texture to feel like real things.
2. **Low-barrier is the message and the medium.** If the page itself feels intimidating to read, the message is dead. Generous spacing, plain language, no jargon unexplained.
3. **Differentiate tracks visually.** A fresher doesn't know what "Quant" means versus "Low Latency". The cards must signal which kind of person each track is for, not just list a name and club.
4. **Registration is two screens, not a wizard.** Click a track → fill a short form → done. Edits live on a dashboard.
5. **DevClub IITD is the only way in.** No fallback auth. Treat the sign-in step as part of the brand, not as friction.
